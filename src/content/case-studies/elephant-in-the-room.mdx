<section class="cine-sec" id="overview">
  <header><span class="cine-num">01</span><h2>Project overview</h2></header>
  <div>
    <p class="cine-lede">The Elephant in the Room helps a team say the hard thing, safely, and then choose what to do about it together.</p>
    <p>Coworkers join from their own phones by scanning a QR code. There is no account and no email. A facilitator runs the session from a laptop.</p>
    <p>The game has three rounds: speak up, solve together, and make a difference.</p>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="cine-sec" id="problem">
  <header><span class="cine-num">02</span><h2>The problem</h2></header>
  <div>
    <p class="cine-lede">My team has real issues. I built an app where people can raise their grievances at work anonymously.</p>
    <p>The app turns those grievances into use cases the team solves together.</p>
    <h3>Who it's for</h3>
    <ol class="cine-flow cols-2">
      <li><b>Players</b><span>Coworkers on their own phones, in a room together. Joining takes seconds.</span></li>
      <li><b>The facilitator</b><span>One person running the session. They need to stay with the room, not babysit the screen.</span></li>
    </ol>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="cine-sec" id="rounds">
  <header><span class="cine-num">03</span><h2>The three rounds</h2></header>
  <div>
    <ol class="cine-flow">
      <li><b>1. Speak Up</b><span>Everyone answers six questions without names. Any question can be skipped. The last one asks what the team does well.</span></li>
      <li><b>2. Solve Together</b><span>Players are placed on teams. Each team gets a workplace scenario and a countdown, submits a solution, then awards badges to other teams' ideas.</span></li>
      <li><b>3. Make a Difference</b><span>The room sees its top concerns and votes for three commitments. Each gets an owner and a 30-day follow-up.</span></li>
    </ol>
    <p>Points reward teamwork: 5 for submitting a solution and 10 for each badge from another team. Complaining earns nothing.</p>
  </div>

<section class="cine-sec" id="decisions">
  <header><span class="cine-num">04</span><h2>Design decisions</h2></header>
  <div>
    <h3>Safe, and honest about it</h3>
    <p>There are no names anywhere. Every question can be skipped, and the facilitator can hide any answer. I chose not to promise total anonymity. The app says answers are shown without names, because I can't promise more than that.</p>
    <h3>Sample players are always labeled</h3>
    <p>The facilitator can fill the room with practice players to rehearse. Every simulated answer is tagged "Simulated", so it can never be mistaken for a real one.</p>
    <h3>Teams are assigned for you</h3>
    <p>The first version let people choose their team. I changed it so each phone is simply told which team it's on, and to find the others and sit together. Players don't choose their team. Assigned teams make every group work together and leave no room for bias.</p>
    <h3>A calmer facilitator dashboard</h3>
    <p>The first dashboard felt like too much to take in, so I cut it down to one column.</p>
    <ol class="cine-flow cols-2">
      <li><b>Three numbers</b><span>Players joined, finished, and answers.</span></li>
      <li><b>One "Just in" line</b><span>It replaced a scrolling feed.</span></li>
      <li><b>Themes that open and close</b><span>Only the biggest theme is open at first.</span></li>
      <li><b>Three actions per answer</b><span>Discuss, Hide, and More.</span></li>
    </ol>
    <p>Rarely used controls (practice players, presenter view, reset) sit behind one "More tools" button.</p>
    <h3>No accounts for players</h3>
    <p>An early version needed people to be invited, and a coworker couldn't take part. I moved the game to its own site, so players only need a link, and only I need a passcode.</p>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="cine-sec" id="solution">
  <header><span class="cine-num">05</span><h2>The solution</h2></header>
  <div>
    <p class="cine-lede">One link for players, one passcode for the facilitator, and a screen for each.</p>
    <p>The video above plays the game on a phone with sample answers, from the welcome screen to the badge voting. The screens below come from the live game.</p>
    <p>The game works on phones and laptops. I checked its layouts from a small 320 pixel phone to a large desktop, in light and dark. Players can choose their text size and turn motion down. Every control shows a clear focus outline, and no meaning depends on colour alone.</p>

<section class="cine-sec" id="outcome">
  <header><span class="cine-num">06</span><h2>Outcomes &amp; reflection</h2></header>
  <div>
    <p class="cine-lede">Seven coworkers played.</p>
    <p>They had an open, flowing conversation about the grievances and blockers they face at work. Now they can work out together how to help each other get past those blockers.</p>
    <h3>The three commitments the team chose</h3>
    <p>The game offers commitments drawn from the team's top themes. These are the three the team chose:</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Send a short written decision summary within 24 hours of every key meeting.</li>
      <li>Pick one recurring process pain point each sprint and fix or retire it.</li>
      <li>Open every planning meeting with a two-minute "what are we missing?" round.</li>
    </ul>
    <h3>What I learned</h3>
    <p>AI is a useful way to start team bonding.</p>
  </div>
</section>

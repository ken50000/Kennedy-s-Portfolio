## The problem

Most teams know where the friction is, but few say it out loud. Speaking up feels risky, and in a normal retro the loudest voice sets the agenda.

I wanted one session where quiet people can speak as easily as confident ones, complaints turn into solutions, and the team leaves with something specific that they picked themselves.

## Who it's for

- **Players:** coworkers on their own phones, in a room together. Joining has to take seconds, with no account and no email.
- **The facilitator:** one person running the session from a laptop. They need to stay with the room, not babysit the screen.

## The idea: three rounds

**1. Speak Up.** Everyone answers six questions on their phone, without names. Any question can be skipped. The last question asks what the team does really well, so the session doesn't end on a complaint. Answers sort into seven themes that the facilitator can regroup, pin, or hide.

**2. Survival Challenge.** Players are placed on teams automatically. Each team gets a workplace scenario, a mission, three discussion prompts, and a countdown. There are five scenarios, from a miscommunication to a broken process. Teams submit one solution, then award badges to other teams' ideas. Points reward teamwork: 5 for a solution and 10 for each badge from another team. Complaining earns nothing.

**3. Resolution Room.** The room sees its top five concerns and the best solutions, then votes for three commitments. Each commitment gets an owner, a status, and a 30-day follow-up date, with a summary the team can copy.

## Design decisions

**Safe, and honest about it.** There are no names anywhere. Every question can be skipped, and the facilitator can hide any answer. I chose not to promise total anonymity. The app says answers are shown without names, because I can't promise more than that.

**Sample players are always labeled.** The facilitator can fill the room with practice players to rehearse. Every simulated answer is tagged "Simulated", so it can never be mistaken for a real one.

**Teams are assigned for you.** The first version let people choose their team. I changed it so each phone is simply told which team it's on, and to find the others and sit together.

**A calmer facilitator dashboard.** The first dashboard felt like too much to take in, so I cut it down to one column:

- three numbers: players joined, finished, and answers
- one "Just in" line instead of a scrolling feed
- themes that open and close, with only the biggest one open at first
- three actions on each answer: Discuss, Hide, and More
- rarely used controls (practice players, presenter view, reset) behind one "More tools" button

**No accounts for players.** An early version needed people to be invited, and a coworker couldn't take part. I moved the game to its own site, so players only need a link, and only I need a passcode.

## Accessibility and screen sizes

- Light, dark, or automatic look, three text sizes, and a reduced-motion setting, all chosen by the viewer.
- A visible focus outline on every control, and focus moves to the page heading on each new screen.
- Controls are at least 44 pixels tall, and colour is never the only way to tell themes apart.
- The layouts were checked from a small 320 pixel phone to a 1920 pixel desktop, in light and dark. The dashboard overflowed on small phones, and I fixed it.
- Before the session, the whole flow was run from start to finish with automated tests, with a facilitator and several players at once.

## The problem

The Workforce Planning Portal helps store leads build schedules. It ran on legacy design standards that couldn't
keep up with modern frameworks, or with planners juggling labor demand, associate availability, and last-minute
changes.

- The legacy UI was fragmented and inconsistent
- Design and engineering had no single source of truth
- Planners had little visibility into scheduling gaps as they happened

## My approach

I used the components and standards of Walmart's new design system, LD3.5, as the foundation, and built custom
pieces only where scheduling really needed them. The goal was consistency across the system without losing the
edge cases that make scheduling hard.

- Interviewed People Leads who manage "borrowed" associates from other teams
- Prioritized the scheduling dashboard as the highest-impact screen
- Placed Sidekick AI inside the workflow, where a conflict happens, instead of bolting it on

## Key decisions

Every feature aimed to lower the mental load of a fast, high-stakes decision.

- A central dashboard that shows demand and coverage gaps at a glance
- Real-time adjustments as the day unfolds
- AI-recommended fixes right at the point of a conflict

## Outcome

The portal now runs on the new design system. Feedback has been positive on clarity, consistency, and real-time
visibility. Onboarding came up as the next area to invest in.

## What's next

## Overview

Ani is an animanga social platform where fans track what they watch and read, discover new series, and talk
about them with other fans. I designed the core experience and built a design system that let the team iterate
quickly. Working closely with engineering, we launched an MVP that reached **10,000+ downloads in its first month**.

## Discovery

To learn how fans track, discover, and talk about anime and manga today, I ran a four-week discovery sprint:

- 20+ interviews with fans at different levels of engagement
- A review of the community spaces they already use: Reddit, Discord, and niche forums
- A competitive and adjacent-product analysis, summarized in a SWOT

The research pointed to three gaps: discovery was fragmented, tracking tools were inconsistent, and few products
were built around community.

## Sitemap and personas

Research showed a core audience of highly engaged fans who wanted better ways to track, discover, and connect.
Existing platforms were built for consuming content, not for talking about it. Personas built from those insights
shaped the sitemap, which put list management, discovery, and community at the center.

## Design system

To support rapid iteration, I introduced slot-based component patterns. Content could adapt across features while
the structure stayed consistent, which cut down on one-off components. As the system matured, I refactored the
more complex components to scale with the product.

## Reflection

Building Ani from zero taught me to balance speed with long-term scale. Setting up the design system early made
iteration faster later. It also showed me where more structure and documentation would have helped other people
adopt it. Next, I'd refine onboarding, deepen personalization, and connect discovery more tightly to community.
